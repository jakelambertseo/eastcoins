Rewards are the Armory's language: Brass, finishes, titles, name effects and bean skins. The season knife at tier 50 and the season bean at tier 25 are the only two things that never come from a case, so the pass is the only way to own them. A tier is roughly one good online session or one solo evening; the whole track is about six weeks of regular play, which is the season.

The Lot Files. While everyone was watching the game, the bots took the maps. Bot Dude #1 runs the Lot, Tin Man has the Docks, Clanker holds the Rooftops and Mr Roboto's squad has dug into the Compound. Sixteen missions, four maps, four bosses. Every mission is a mode that already exists with one twist on it; every star is season XP; every chapter ends with a title nobody else can buy.

Stars: one for finishing, two for beating the par time, three for no deaths. Pace: the first three missions of a chapter unlock together; the boss opens when you hold six stars in the chapter. Replays keep your best.

Five drills, sixty seconds each, played in the game's own engine on an empty range: same sensitivity, same guns, same crosshair as a match. Bronze, silver and gold per drill pay season XP once each; a daily warm-up (any three drills) pays 50 XP. Every drill has its own all-time and weekly board.

Already in the game

  • Bots, maps, modes, parkour, XP, Brass, levels, skins and the daily challenges — every mission above is a practice round with a rule twist, so the engine needs no new systems to play them.
  • Practice runs in the browser with the real rules file; a mission is practice mode plus a modifier (bot count, bot skill, allowed guns, a timer, a zone, a boss bean with more health).
  • The season pass is a ledger: one table of season XP per Twitch account, one of claimed tiers. The site already pays Brass idempotently; the same shape pays tiers.

What has to be built

  • A mission runner in the page: load a mission's rules, run it, judge stars, report the result. ~2–3 days with the sixteen missions written.
  • The aim range: an empty map, targets, five drill scripts, a board endpoint. ~2 days.
  • The pass: season XP sources wired into the round report, the challenges and the solo reports; the track page; claiming. ~1–2 days.
  • A season's rewards: a knife, a bean and a handful of finishes and titles drawn for it.

The honest limit

  • Solo scores are reported by the page, not judged by the room server the way online rounds are. Someone who wants to can forge a gold medal.
  • So solo play should pay season XP and titles, never Brass directly, and the pass should cap how much of a tier solo play can fill in a day — then a forged score buys a cosmetic a day early and nothing else.
  • Online play stays the only source of Brass and the fast lane for the pass, which keeps the rooms full.

Order, if you want it

  • 1. Aim lab — smallest, standalone, and the boards give the regulars something to fight over at once.
  • 2. The pass — turns everything already earned into a track with an end date.
  • 3. Campaign — the biggest piece; chapter one alone (four missions on the Lot) would be a fine first drop.
