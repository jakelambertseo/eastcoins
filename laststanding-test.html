Every player has exactly the same chance. The order is drawn before the first knockout, so the countdown is playback rather than a live draw.

How it works

  • One buy-in, one winner, no decisions. Nobody plays anything — you sit down and watch. That is the point: it is ninety seconds of pure spectacle you can fire off whenever the room is quiet, and it needs nothing from anyone except turning up.
  • Everyone's odds are the same, whatever the size. Twelve players means a 1 in 12 shot at twelve buy-ins. The whole elimination order is drawn from the committed seed before the first knockout, so nothing is decided while people watch.
  • Finishing order is worth showing even though it pays nothing. Going out first is funny, going out second is agony, and both are better than a result that just names a winner.
  • It scales the opposite way to Russian Roulette. That game is a small steady win with a rare hit; this is a rare big win and a steady drip of losses. Having both means the casino covers both moods.

Rules of the road

  • On the site only. A lobby on the casino floor with a join countdown. No chat commands and no bot announcements.
  • Buy-ins are taken on joining, so the pot on screen is real money and nobody can back out once the round starts.
  • Minimum three players, or it is a coin flip with a longer animation. If the countdown ends short, everyone is refunded and the lobby reopens.
  • Closing the tab changes nothing. The order is already decided and the payout runs server-side, so you can win one while you are on the Sports page.
  • Winnings count toward the hourly cap, which means this table has to be added to hourlyNet() or it escapes the cap entirely. A twelve-player round at 20 each pays 240, well past the 300 an hour ceiling on its own.
