Party Games

Small multiplayer games on eastcoin.vip, played for ZCoins, in the EastScape look. A quick lobby, a game that takes about a minute or two, a pot, and a ladder to climb. Some are Fall Guys / Mario Party (shove, hop, race and survive). Some are table games (bluff, bank and gamble on a sealed fuse). A few are solo arcade cabinets with daily high scores. Two of them are playable below against bots: Pushover and Hot Potato.

The Party Hall

One page, /?view=party, built from the kit's windows. Quick play drops you in the next table that is filling. Below that is every open table with its seats, its buy-in and its countdown. A table starts when its clock runs out with at least two seated, the same way the PvP tables start, so nobody waits for a full room. A private room gets a four-letter code to paste in chat. Watching is free, and the players and the people watching share one room, so the hall never looks empty.

Play: Pushover

Sumo meets Jump Showdown. Everyone starts on a velvet ring in the dark. Shove to send people flying, and hop to dodge a shove or the sweeper. Every 8 seconds the outer band flashes red and drops away. At 18 seconds a striped sweeper comes out of the middle and speeds up. The last one on the ring takes the pot. A round is about a minute, and if you're knocked off early you watch the rest, so it is never dead time. The bots go for whoever is nearest the edge, and that includes you.

Why it works

Two buttons, and you get it in five seconds. Chaos makes it funny: being shoved off by the sweeper while you're busy shoving someone else is the joke. The shrinking ring means nobody can hide, and the cooldowns mean nobody can spam.

Play: Hot Potato

A greed game on a sealed fuse. Holding the potato earns 10 points a second, but they only count once you pass it. Passing banks them. When it blows, whoever is holding it (or about to catch it) loses everything unbanked and 25 from the bank. Nobody knows the fuse: it is drawn from a seed whose hash is shown before the round and revealed after it, like every casino game here. A catch takes 0.8 s before you can throw, and you can't throw straight back. So passing late is an attack, and the bots will do it to whoever is leading. Five rounds, and the highest bank takes the pot.

Hot Potato

Six at the table, 20 ZC each (pretend chips). Highest bank after five rounds takes the pot.

When it's in your hands: click a player or press 2–6 to pass. Nobody is shown a fuse clock, because nobody has one.

Why this one is safe for real ZCoins: a script knows exactly what you know. The fuse is sealed, and the 0.8 s catch swamps any reaction-time edge. What wins is reading the table: how greedy to be, and who to hand it to when it's getting late. That is the shape every coin table needs (see ZCoins and bots).

Board Night

The Mario Party one, cut to a lunch break: 4 players, 8 turns. Roll a die (sealed like every seed), walk the loop, and land on coins, a trap, a shop or a chance space. CASINO sells a crown for 20 board coins somewhere on the loop, then moves it. After every second turn the board stops and everyone plays one of the other games (a 40-second Pushover, a three-pass Potato, one Light Cycles round) for board coins. Most crowns wins, and coins break a tie. It is the one game here that takes longer than five minutes, so it gets its own scheduled table ("Board Night, 8 PM") rather than quick play.

ZCoins and bots

This is the hard part, and Red Light already worked it out: when real ZCoins ride on a game, anything a script does better than a person is a leak. A script never misses a shove window and never steers Light Cycles into a wall. So the games split into three kinds, each with its own money rule.

Rankings and high scores

The part that turns a game into a habit. Everything here uses machinery the site already has: badges, the bell, the activity feed and profile tabs.

A rating per gameElo-style, seeded at 1,200. Multiplayer results count as a set of head-to-heads, so finishing 2nd of 8 still gains. Quick play matches by rating once the hall is busy enough.
A monthly seasonAn overall party ladder on the hall (as drawn above), reset each month. The top three get a title in the store's title slot ("Party Animal", "Unshoveable", "Potato Lord") until next season.
Daily high scoresThe arcade plays a daily seed, so everyone has the same run. A new daily record goes to the ticker. The record for the week, month and all time stays on each game's own page.
BadgesNew ones in _badges.js: 🥊 most Pushover wins this week, 🥔 never blown up in 20 games, 🎲 called liar right five times running. Drawn next to names like the others.
A Party tab on profilesNext to Picks, Casino and Music: rating per game, record, net, best finish and a favourite game, built from the results table.
Feed, not chatWins and records go to the activity feed and the ticker (type party). The bell only pings when your record falls or a table you queued for starts. Nothing goes to Twitch chat.

How it runs

Not on D1 polling. A Pushover round is 8 people sending inputs 20 times a second, and a 1.5 s poll would make it unplayable and spend reads by the million (see the D1 note in the handoff). The shape is the one EastScape already uses: one Durable Object per room, over a WebSocket.

PieceWhatCost, said out loud
RoomA PartyRoom Durable Object in a new eastcoin-party worker (or a class in the EastScape worker). It runs the lobby clock, the seed and the simulation, then settles.One object per live table. It sleeps (WebSocket hibernation) between messages, so an empty lobby costs almost nothing.
Action gamesAn authoritative sim at 20 Hz. The page sends inputs (move, shove, hop) and draws 10 Hz snapshots with interpolation. The same rules file runs in the page for prediction, the Red Light pattern.8 players × 20 inputs/s × 60 s ≈ 9,600 messages in a round, which Cloudflare bills at 20:1 as ~480 requests. 10 snapshots/s out to 8 + watchers is free. 100 rounds a day is about 50k requests, which is nothing.
Table gamesEvent-driven: a pass, a bid, a pick. No tick at all.A few hundred messages a game.
MoneyAt settle, the room calls a Pages function with the cron-key pattern. Buy-ins are WAGER_DEBIT keyed PARTY:BUY:<room>:<user>, and payouts PAYOUT_CREDIT keyed PARTY:PAY:<entry>. That's the PvP path, idempotent per entry.D1 is touched only at join and at settle: 2–3 writes per player per game, and one results row.
HallThe lobby list is pushed over one socket to a PartyHall object, not polled.One connection per open Hall tab, with hibernation. No reads.
RailEvery stage sits left of the chat rail. Nothing floats over it (Twitch's obscured check), and below 980 px the stage takes the strip the drawer leaves, the same rule as the Gold Button.—

What to build first

1 · The room and the HallThe PartyRoom object, the lobby clock, buy-in and settle, and the Hall page. Ship it with Hot Potato: a coin table, event-driven, no physics, and a good test of the money path.
2 · Red Light moves inIt is already built and tested. Re-homing it in the Hall gives the hall two coin tables on day one.
3 · PushoverThe first action game: the 20 Hz sim, prediction and snapshots. Start it as a free / titles table while you decide the skill-table rule.
4 · Ladder, badges, Party tabOnce there are results to rank.
5 · Then the lineupLiar's Dice and Light Cycles next (both cheap once the room exists), then the Arcade, then Board Night, which needs three minigames before it's worth playing.
