Character stats

A Character button on the Equipment tab opens one window with every number the game uses about you: what you hit for and how often, how hard you are to hurt, how fast you work, what you find, and what's buffing you right now. Each stat shows how close it is to its cap, and clicking one shows where it comes from and where to get more, which is what turns a sheet of numbers into something to chase. Everything marked new is a stat the game doesn't have yet (resistances, critical hits, blocking) and says where it would come from; every other number below is real, worked out by the game's own rules for the example character.

The chase

The bottom of the window can fold out into this: every capped stat in the game, how far along you are, green when it's maxed. This is the Diablo part, the reason to keep farming after the levels are done: "capped resistances", "max work speed", "30% damage taken". The game already caps eighteen of these; the new ones join the same list.

The new stats

Three things the sheets you sent have that the game doesn't. Corrected 1 October: the first version of this page said monsters past 90 hit calvin 95% of the time whatever he wears, but that compared a level-82 player with level-110 monsters. Measured at level, defence works up to 99 (at-level players are hit 20-30%). What is true is that monster attack stops rising at 126 past level 100, so a 99 in Singularity is hit about 20% by everything from 92 to 115, and more armour after that changes nothing. Resistances give the top of the game a second kind of defence to chase.

Resistances use what existsThe Frozen Reach's cold already hurts anyone without a ward, 25 a second. That becomes frost resistance: a ward is 75%, the cap. The Underforge's heat, the Deep End's storm and the Star Archive's void (mockup 4) each get the same, and so do monsters' elemental hits from level 100 up.
The Gem Sorter feeds themAn element gem in a weapon socket hits that element harder, as it does in the bag today; in an armour socket (mockup 3) it resists it. Same gems, same rolls, a choice of where to put them.
Nothing old changesEvery number that exists keeps its formula and its cap. The new ones start at zero for everybody (crit at a base 5%) and only matter on the maps built to need them.

Building it

  • Every "today" number is already a function in the rules file the page loads: bonusOf, attackRollOf, defenceRollOf, maxHitOf, swingMsOf, maxHpOf, stepMsOf, fxOf with OUT_CAP, petFx, gemBonus, charmOf, tkOn, hitChance. The window calls them on the character the page already has. No server work and no new message at all.
  • The "where it comes from" lists are the same functions' inputs read one at a time (each worn item's fx, the pet, the gem bag, the meal, the drink, the charm, the gadgets, the achievement milestones), so they can never disagree with the total.
  • "Where to get more" is a lookup over the item, pet, gem and gadget tables for anything that adds to that stat, filtered to what the player could use, with a link to its wiki page.
  • The new stats are bigger work, and each belongs to a mockup: resistances with the endgame maps (4) and sockets (3), crit with Ascendancies (2) and masterwork (3), block with the Juggernaut (2). The window can ship first with the row hidden until the stat exists.
Things to decide: whether the window shows monster hit chances (honest, and it shows defence stops mattering past 90, which may be the thing to fix first); the resistance cap (75%, as Path of Exile); whether crit is worth adding at all or the game stays crit-free; and whether other players' sheets are visible by default.
