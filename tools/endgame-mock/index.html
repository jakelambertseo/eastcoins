Past 110: four places, not one

The endgame isn't one map going deeper. It's four separate places, each off a different corner of the world, each with its own look, its own rule, its own monsters, its own boss and its own things to chase. Their bands overlap, so at any level from 110 up there are two or three to choose from, and a group that's bored of one has somewhere else to go. Between them they hold every 110 resource from mockup 1, the Relic shards and boss pieces for mockup 3, and the hardest fights the Ascendancies of mockup 2 are built for.

Where they are

Each one hangs off the end of a road that already exists, so all four are a walk from somewhere people already go: the forest past the Primeval Ridge, a diving bell off the Boardwalk's long pier, the stair under the Thunderhead, and the star-bridge at the top of the Frostspire. The world map gets four new corners instead of one longer road.

Side by side

The four are built to pull in different directions, so no one weapon, element or skill does everything. Each is weak to a different element, which gives every one of the elemental gems from the Gem Sorter a map where it's the best stone you own.

The Mountain Rumbles

Kept from the first draft, because it's the part everybody can join. Once a day at a random hour the Thunderhead's south ridge splits, and the things that live under it come up: Ash Wraiths, Obsidian Golems and Molten Titans, scaled to whoever is standing there (combat 50 and up). Plug the crack with ore, the Flood's sandbags in reverse, and at the end the Heart of the Mountain pushes up through it for one last fight. Everybody who helped gets a few Relic shards, which is how a level-70 player first holds something from past 110.

Like every new event: once a day, never in the Yard, on the Flood's raid code (which already survives a restart).

How long

On mockup 1's curve, 110 is 38.7 million XP and 120 is 104 million. The best melee on the server is at 3.3 million today, so the first of these maps is roughly twelve times as far away as everything they've done so far. Four places to spend those hours, instead of one, is the point of this take.

Building it

  • Four maps on the map pipeline, each on its own ground and props (moss and ruin; sunken streets and coral; basalt and lava; marble and starlight). About the work of the Frozen Reach each, so they can ship one at a time in band order, Elderwood first, with The Road Ahead teasing the rest.
  • Twelve monsters and four bosses, each with four facings and walk, attack and death, plus the Mountain Rumbles' three and the Heart.
  • The four rules are deliberately small, and each reuses something: the Elderwood's daily layout is three walk grids picked by the date; the Deep End's tide is the Flood's water overlay on a clock; the Underforge's heat is one number per player on the existing tick; the Star Archive's bridges are tiles switching walkable on a timer, the same way the Flood's water slows people.
  • The Leviathan is the Ice Wyrm's schedule (once a day, random hour, never 12 am to 2 pm) with raid parts; the other three are hourly bosses in the Captain Claw shape.
  • Performance: nothing here adds a poll or a new message. The tide and the bridges are clocks both sides already know, so the page draws them without asking; heat rides the state the page already gets.
Things to decide: the four (keep, swap or add one: a desert tomb past the Golden Sands was the fifth idea); the Underforge's way in (through the held Foundry or a lift off the Thunderhead); whether the Leviathan is the one boss that needs a healer, or the Archivist too; and the names.
