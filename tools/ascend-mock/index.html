Ascendancies

The Road Ahead's rumour, made real: "Past the top, you choose what you become." When one of your fighting skills (Melee, Archery or Magic) reaches 100, its Ascendancy trial opens. Pass it and you choose one of two paths for that way of fighting. Every level from 101 to 120 in that skill is a point in the path's tree, so the long road past 99 (mockup 1) is also the road to your build. Each path has three abilities and a signature move that go off on their own, in an order you set, a look, and a choice near the top that changes how it plays. One of the six is a healer, because the hardest dungeons will need one.

How it works

UnlockA fighting skill at 100 opens that style's trial. Melee 100 opens the Melee trial, and so on.
One path per stylePass it and choose one of its two paths. You can ascend in all three styles over time, one path each; the one that counts is the style of the weapon in your hand.
24 pointsOne at 100, one for every level from 101 to 120 (21 in all), and one more for each harder trial at 110 and 120. The tree costs 31, so nobody takes everything.
An ability orderThree attacks and the signature move, each on its own cooldown, go off by themselves: every swing uses the first one in your order that is ready. The only thing you do is set the order. Everyone gets the plain three as their style levels (1, 30, 60); an Ascendancy turns them into its own.
No new keysNothing to press, so food and potions stay on 1-4 where they are. A fight plays the same with one hand on the mouse.
RespecMoving points inside your path is free once a week, then tickets. Changing to the other path means taking the trial again.
A lookAn aura in the path's colour, its emblem by your name and on your profile, and a title.

Choosing

After the trial, one window and one decision. This is the Melee one; Archery and Magic look the same with their own two.

The Melee AscendancyChoose one. You can change later, but it means the trial again.

Choosing costs nothing. Your first point (from reaching 100) is ready to spend in the tree straight away.

The six paths

Two ways to fight for each style, and they really are different: one for a single big target, one for a crowd; one that stays standing, one that hits first. Every path also has a choice of two keystones at the top of its tree, so two players on the same path can still play it differently.

The ability order

Kept simple on purpose. Fights are already a swing on a timer; now each swing looks along your order and uses the first ability that's off cooldown, or a plain swing if none is. There's nothing to press and nothing to miss, so a player on a laptop trackpad hits as hard as one with a keyboard; the skill is in the order. Put the big hit first and it goes off whenever it's back; put a cheap one first and it eats the swings the big one could have had. The server runs the order, so it's the same for everyone, and an order is four small numbers per player. Try it below: pick a path and watch it fight, then drag the slots (or use the arrows) to change the order.

Healers are needed

A healer is only worth being if a group can't win without one. Today a party heals itself with food, so the hard dungeons (the Mythic runs, mockup 5) are built so that food alone can't keep up, and a Mender can:

Damage on everyoneBosses pulse the whole room every few seconds. Nobody can eat fast enough to outheal it for long; Mend, Chain Mend and Sanctuary can.
Food has a cooldownIn a Mythic run you can eat once every 6 seconds, not every tick. Potions too.
Curses only Cleanse removesBleeds, poisons and the Ice Man's freeze stack on a party and only wear off, unless a Mender washes them off.
A tank and a healerThe top Mythic levels ask for a party of four with a Juggernaut to hold the boss (Iron Will) and a Mender to keep everyone up. The party window shows the roles before you go in.
Healing is countedThe party meter gets a Healing tab, the run report a "kept everyone alive" line, and the hiscores a Healing board, so the healer is seen.
No clicking on peopleHeals go to whoever is most hurt within reach, so there's nothing to target and no mis-clicks in a crowd. Ward and Mend never pick a full-health ally.
PartyThe Deep Vault · Mythic 8 · needs a tank and a healer

The trials

The Hall of Ascension stands at the top of the Tower, above the House. Each trial is solo, about five minutes, and tests the thing the style is about. Fail and you walk out: no death, no bill, try again in ten minutes.

Balance

QuestionThe answer in this design
How much stronger is an ascended player?A full 24 points is worth about +20% in the style's own fights, about the step from one weapon tier to the next. Enough to feel, not enough that a level 100 without a path can't keep up in a party.
The keystonesEach one changes how the path plays and pays for it (Unbreakable deals 10% less; Glass Edge takes 15% more). None is simply "more damage".
The WildernessSignature moves work, but stuns last half as long on players, and keystones' damage parts count half. Nobody ascends into being unkillable in PvP.
AbilitiesThe plain three are worth about a weapon tier, and everyone gets them without doing anything. A good order is worth a few percent over a bad one: enough to matter at the top, never enough to lock anyone out. Ascended versions are stronger and on the same cooldowns, and there are never more than four in the order.
The healerA Mender's damage is about two-thirds of the other paths', and it never heals more than a party loses: heals can't overheal, and Ward can't stack. In everyday fights a Mender is welcome but not needed; in the Mythic runs, needed.
BossesEffects that work off a monster's health (Siphon, Executioner) are capped on bosses, so raids stay a group fight.
Respec costFree once a week inside a path; after that 25,000 tickets, doubling each time in the same week. Switching path: the trial again.

Building it

  • Needs mockup 1 first: points come from levels 101 to 120, so the road past 99 is the foundation.
  • The tree is data: nodes, costs and effects in the rules file, and the effects are mostly hooks the combat code already has (damage, accuracy, crit, damage taken, healing, cooldowns).
  • The ability order: one more message ("my order is 3, 1, 2, 4", only sent when it changes), a cooldown per ability per player, and each swing picking the first ready one. Cheaper than keys: no message per use at all. The plain three for every style come first and work at every level, so the bar is in everybody's hands long before 100.
  • The six paths' abilities and signature moves one at a time: most are a bigger hit, a splash (the Cleave code), a timed effect on a monster or a player (Mark, Ward, Dread), or a heal. Earthshaker reuses the Ice Man's slam. Healing is new to the game's combat and needs "most-hurt ally within reach" and a no-overheal rule.
  • The trials are three small solo rooms on the Tower's roof, built like the crypt rooms with a scripted Examiner.
  • Art (PixelLab): six emblems (drafts here), six auras, the Examiner in three styles, the Hall, and a little animation for each signature move.
  • Cost on the server: small. A few more numbers in each hit and one more cooldown per player.
Things to decide: the six names (Juggernaut, Duelist, Deadeye, Trickshot, Mender, Hexer); the keys (abilities on 1-4 and food on Q/E/R/F); whether a player may ascend in all three styles or only one; and where the Hall goes (the Tower's roof here).
