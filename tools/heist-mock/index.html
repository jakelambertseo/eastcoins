🗝️ The Heist

The thieving world event from the thieving mock, built out. The Jackpot Thief already gives the world a chase, so this one is a job: once a day the House moves the takings into the Vault, Marla Nine-Fingers puts a crew together, and for ten minutes anyone who turns up is on it. Thieves crack the boxes, low levels lift the keycards, fighters hold off the guards, and whoever is quick on their feet carries the sacks out. It happens in the Casino and the Vault, never in the Yard, and nobody dies.

The shape

A fourth daily event, and the first one where Thieving is the main skill.Shooting Stars is for miners, Wanted! and the Jackpot Thief are for fighters. The Heist is the one thieves have been missing, and it still has a paid job for every level and every build: a level-3 can lift a Copper keycard, a pure fighter can hold a guard, an Agility main can run sacks out through the service hatch. It goes in the day planner as a fourth window (EVDAY's 12:00 to midnight is cut into four instead of three, with the same 30-minute margin), so it is still once a day and never closer than an hour to another event.

1 · The tip-off

Ten minutes' warning, like the Shooting Stars. CASINO says it to everyone; Marla says it to anyone who walks into the Guild, along with the plan and who is already in. There is nothing to sign and no permit for the night: the Guild's 15,000-ticket permit is for its marks, and the Heist is meant to bring people who have never been in. Turning up is joining.

2 · Case the joint

For three minutes the Vault stays shut and the House's staff walk the Casino floor with keycards on their belts. They are marks, using the Guild's own formula, so nothing new has to be balanced: Copper is the Apprentice Lifter's level, Silver the Grifter's, Gold The Fixer's. A lift puts the card in the crew's pool, not your bag, so a level-3's Copper card opens boxes for everyone. Every card opens more boxes: the rows open at 3 cards, and each card after that adds two boxes to its row, up to 12. A crew that cases well gets a bigger Vault.

The Guild's own formula: min(90%, 55% + 2% × (you − the mark) + steal), 2.4 s a try, a fail stuns you. Each member of staff carries 4 cards, refilled every 20 s, so a crowd cannot strip the floor in ten seconds. A lift pays 4 picks of that mark's Guild XP.

Why cards, not a door with health

The first draft (EASTSCAPE-DRAFTS: "vault doors with health, guard waves") made it a fight, and a fight is what Wanted! and the raids already are. Cards make the first three minutes a thieving job a level-1 can do, and they make the size of the Vault the crowd's own doing: a quiet night opens one row, a busy one all three and the House Box.

They also give the high levels a reason to wait for the low ones. Kellzifer can't open the Gold row alone fast enough; he needs the clerks' Copper cards lifted first to get anything to carry.

3 · The Vault

At 3:00 the back stairs open. For the length of the job the Vault's own monsters step out (the Wardens, Pit Bosses, Coin Hoards and the Last Dealer: nobody farming there loses a fight they didn't choose, and they get the ten-minute warning too) and the room becomes three box walls and a door. Each walkway is lined with a wall of boxes; click one to crack it. a crack is a timed action like a pick, and a success hands you a sack. You carry one sack at a time, slowly, up the stairs to Odile's cart, and come back for another. House Security walks the rows with a lamp. If a guard's light finds you carrying, he walks you up to the Casino floor, and the sack stays on the Vault floor for anyone to pick up.

The one choice in the job: quiet or quick. A crew of careful thieves never sees lockdown and empties less; a greedy one fills the alarm by minute seven. Your steal bonus (the Ditched set, the Lockpick Set, potions, pets) counts here as it does in the Guild.

Carrying, guards and the ways out
A sackOne at a time, and heavy−30% walk speed at Agility 1, easing to −10% at 99. No teleports, no bank and no trading while you hold one. Drop it (or get walked out) and it sits on the floor for anyone.
GuardsCan't be killed, can be held2, plus one for every six crew, up to 8. They walk the rows with a lamp; light on someone carrying means caught. Any hit stuns one for 3 s; Combat 40+ holds it for 8 s, and a held guard sees nobody.
CaughtWalked upstairs, nothing lostNo damage, no death, no hospital bill. You lose the sack you were carrying (it stays down there) and ten seconds' walk. That is the whole punishment, so a level-3 can be a runner.
The hatchThe short way outAgility 30 opens the service hatch on the Vault's east side, straight into the alley beside Odile's cart: about a third of the walk, and no guard on it. It is the first shortcut that pays someone else's skill.

4 · Lockdown

If the alarm reaches 100%, the job is over early. The doors start closing (60 seconds), the guards turn to the doors, and the Pit Boss walks out of the back and down the rows. Anyone still inside when the doors shut is caught: walked upstairs, the sack left behind. Sacks already at the cart are safe; the House only keeps what is still in the room. If the alarm never fills, the job runs to 10:00 and the doors close the same way, without the Pit Boss.

Why the House Box makes the ending. It is the slowest thing in the room (Thieving 90, shared work like the star's health, 60,000 to the take) and it sits at the back, furthest from both ways out. So the last minute always has the same question: do the 90s stay on the House Box while the alarm climbs, or take what they've got and run? That is the moment people will talk about in chat.

The count

When the doors shut, Odile counts what reached the cart. Half of every sack goes to whoever carried it in; the other half, and the House Box, goes in one crew pot split evenly between everyone who did a job (a lift, a crack, a guard held, a sack carried). Even, not by level, because the Heist is the one event where a low level's job is as needed as a high level's: no Copper cards, no Vault. It is the House's money, like every event pot: nothing comes out of the Jackpot and no bet or ZCoin table is touched.

The cap: 12 boxes × 3 rows at full is 140,400 plus the House Box, so a perfect run is 200,400; the 250,000 cap is there in case the numbers move on dev, never as a target. Per person a busy night is ~4,000-8,000: a good half hour in the Yard, once a day. It is a ticket faucet the size of a raid, so it wants watching in the tracking data (trk:<day>) its first week.

Why the alarm and not a timer alone: a plain 10 minutes would be "everyone forces everything," which is the same click-fast game as the Guild. The alarm makes speed cost something, and it is shared: one greedy forcer fills it for everyone, which is the kind of thing a crowd shouts about in chat, in the good way. The cooling (1% every 10 s nobody slips) means a crew that settles down can win a loud start back.

Building it

What it reusesmedium: about the Jackpot Thief plus the stars
  • The planner: EVDAY deals four events into four windows instead of three. Saved per day like today, so a deploy neither loses nor repeats it.
  • Keycard staff are monsters with pockets: the "pockets in the world" piece from the thieving mock (the Guild's pick formula on a monster). The Heist is the first thing to use it, so it lands with it.
  • Boxes are objects you work like a mark, which is the lockbox piece from the same mock, scaled to one room.
  • The House Box is the shooting star's shared-health object: many people, one bar, scaled by how many are on it.
  • Guards walk a fixed loop of tiles and turn to look; the lamp is a cone check each tick against carriers only. They take hits like the Jackpot Thief (counted, never killed).
  • The sack is one field on the player (C.sack = row), read by the walk speed and refused by the bank, trades and teleports. Dropped sacks are ground items with a 10-s owner window, as drops are now.
  • The Vault's monsters are hidden with a scene flag for the length of the job and come back at the end; the scene's own respawn handles the rest.
  • The count uses the raid's share code, with an even split instead of by damage, and logs a line in the new item log for every sack paid.
Server cost and tests
  • Cost: up to 8 guards walking fixed loops (cheaper than fleeing monsters, which path), up to 37 objects, one alarm number. Less than a raid wave. Nothing runs when it isn't on.
  • Restarts: the alarm, the cards, which boxes are open and the take are saved on the event like the Ice Man's state, so a deploy mid-job picks up where it was.
  • Tests (a tools/eastscape-heist-test.mjs on the real World, storage stubbed): a sack can't be banked, traded or teleported; caught drops it on the floor; the count pays every job evenly and never more than the cap; lockdown at 100%; the House Box pays once; a hidden Vault mob never attacks during the job; nothing touches the Jackpot.
  • Dev first, started from the admin panel. Then once a day, spread out, per the rule for new events.

Decisions

1
Where: the Vault, or a new room?The Vault

The Vault already reads as the House's money and is one floor under the Casino. Its monsters step out for ten minutes and come back. A new room means a new map to draw for something that runs ten minutes a day.

2
Can you die?No

Caught means walked upstairs and the sack left behind. That is what lets every level carry, and it keeps the hospital out of an event that's meant to bring new people into Thieving.

3
The split: carrier half + an even crew pot?Yes

The alternative is by contribution, like the raids by damage, which pays the 90s on the House Box most and the card-lifters least. Even makes the low levels' job worth turning up for. Worth watching whether people "do one job" to collect.

4
The Guild permit: needed?Not for the Heist

Marla vouches for you for the night. It's an advert for the Guild; the permit stays for the marks.

5
Quiet or force: keep the choice?Keep it

It's the one decision in the job and it's what makes the shared alarm matter. Without it the box window is a progress bar.

6
The Yard-chase Heist on the raids pageRetire it

The Jackpot Thief is that chase, live since 30 Sep. This version keeps the name and gives thieves their event. The raids page keeps the Siege Engine and the Flood.

7
Its order in the buildAfter pockets & lockboxes

The thieving mock's two world hooks are most of its parts. Build those, then the Heist is mostly the planner slot, the guards, the sack and the count.
