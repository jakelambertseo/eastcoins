Rolled Loot

Gear that can come out better than plain: a few extra lines in plain words, like "+3% more tickets" or "+2 defence", and casino chips that change those lines. It's Path of Exile and Diablo's idea, sized for EastScape. Today's gear never stops being good: everything in the game stays beatable in plain, reforged gear, and rolls are a bonus on top that grows into a long chase.

How it looks

Four grades, named for the casino and coloured so you can read them across the screen. The same Eclipse sword at each grade:

Plain is every item in the game today, unchanged. Lucky has one extra line, Hot two, Jackpot three. A gold ★ marks a line that rolled the best it can for that item. There are no hidden numbers, no "prefix" or "item level" words, and the most lines an item can have is three, against six in Path of Exile.

What can roll

Two kinds of line. Up lines add accuracy, strength or defence; they are small on purpose, because those are what the game's balance is built on. Sideways lines are the effects worn gear already has today (19 of them, measured), each under the cap the game already enforces. So no line is a new stat, the Character window can show every one, and no amount of stacking passes a cap. Values grow with the item's tier: the range on a Bronze piece and on a Singularity piece are both shown.

Never rolls: ZCoin drop chance (real ZCoins: not a random line, ever). The effects that make a named item what it is also never roll: execute, "nothing attacks you first", no hospital bill, gem find, the Bookie's "other worn gear stronger" and forge odds. Those stay the chase on uniques like the Gambler's Ring and the Coffin Ring.

The chips

Six casino chips, each doing one clear thing. The names are table words, so the casino explains them: you raise, you fold, you hold. They stack to 99 and sit in the bank like anything else.

The Dealer, a new NPC by the Gem Sorter, is where chips are used and where phase 1 starts. Before any chips exist, he re-deals one line of an item for tickets: 5,000, then double each time on the same item (Diablo 4's enchanting). That is the whole system for a casual player: "don't like that line? pay to change it." And it's a ticket sink that the richest players will feed for a perfect roll.

Where rolled gear comes from

Measured: . In EastScape, gear is made, not found, so rolls have to come with crafting, not only from drops (Diablo's model would leave Smithing out of the best part of the game).

Making an item at its requirement level is mostly Plain; making it well above your level is how a smith gets Jackpots. That gives every high Smithing, Fletching and Wizardry level a new reason to exist, and replaces the "masterwork" quality idea from the Relic mockup with one system instead of two (see Decisions).

For casual players

The worry is a wall of numbers on day one. Seven rules keep it away:

1. Nothing rolls below level 30

Bronze and Emerald gear is always Plain. A new player's first month is exactly today's game. Rolls start on Diamond gear and Diamond-tier drops.

2. Plain is always enough

Every monster, boss and dungeon is tuned to Plain gear reforged to +3. Rolled gear makes things faster and comfier; it never becomes the price of entry. Say it in the Dealer's first line.

3. Plain words, three lines at most

"+3% more tickets", "+2 defence". No affix tiers or item levels shown. A ★ for the best possible roll is the only extra symbol.

4. Compare at a glance

Hovering an item shows green ▲ and red ▼ against what you're wearing, the way the Character window already reads gear. The game does the maths.

5. One new verb at a time

Phase 1 has no chips, only "re-deal this line" for tickets. Chips arrive later, one or two at a time, each introduced by the Dealer with a free one.

6. Nothing is ever lost

No chip breaks an item (reforging already has that risk, and that's enough). The worst a chip can do is roll a line you like less.

7. It is a bonus, so it can wait

Someone who ignores the whole system loses about a quarter of a tier of power (a typical roll), not a reason to quit.

Rolled out in three phases

Phase 1 · Lucky

first, about 4 days
  • Gear from Diamond up can come out Lucky (one line).
  • The Dealer re-deals a line for tickets (5k, doubling).
  • No chips yet. Loot highlighting (the QoL pack) makes a Lucky drop glow.

Phase 2 · Hot

2-4 weeks later
  • Hot items (two lines).
  • Chips: Lucky, Shuffle, Raise drop from monsters, raids and Bronny's order; the Dealer sells Lucky and Shuffle.
  • Salvage (Tinkering) turns unwanted rolled gear into chips.

Phase 3 · Jackpot

with the road past 99
  • Jackpot items (three lines).
  • Re-deal, Fold and Hold chips from bosses and Elite diaries.
  • A CASINO line in chat for a perfect ★★★ Jackpot. Relic gear (mockup 3) rolls too.

Where it fits in balance

Measured with the game's own combat formulas, for a melee fighter in a full set of their level's tier with that tier's sword. The bars start near the plain value so the differences show. The red line is the next tier. Reforging to +3 already reaches it, which is today's ceiling over plain gear. Rolls are sized so a perfect set adds most of a tier (+6 of the +7 defence roll a tier gives at 70, +3 of the +4 attack roll), and a typical set about a quarter. Perfect means the best roll on every one of seven pieces, a long chase.

Up lines are small; sideways lines carry the fun

A tier step is about +4 attack roll, +2 max hit and +7 defence roll. A perfect Up line is worth a fraction of that. Most lines go sideways (tickets, rare find, speed, less damage), so a Jackpot changes how you play more than how hard you hit.

The caps are the guard rails

Every sideways line counts toward the cap that already exists (tickets 25%, speed 20%, less damage 30%, rare find 40% and so on). Stacking rolls can reach a cap sooner; it can never pass it. The Character window already shows those bars.

Stacking order: base, reforge, rolls, gems

One budget: reforge levels (about one tier), then rolls (most of a tier when perfect, a quarter when typical), then socketed gems as today. The defence fix in the game plan must land first, or extra defence past 90 means very little.

The economy

The Dealer's re-deal and chip prices are a ticket sink for the richest players, one the sinks mockup found missing. Chips never cost ZCoins and never touch the casino's odds. Rolled gear can be traded, so the best rolls get a price, and the bank and trades carry the roll with the item.

Content stays tuned to plain

Monsters are not re-balanced for rolled gear. If rolls ever become required, they've been sized wrong; the fix is the roll ranges, not the monsters.

What it does to XP

Up lines speed up kills by a few percent at most; ticket and rare-find lines speed up income under the existing caps. Neither touches XP, so it doesn't fight the 2X split.

Decisions

1. Rolls on crafted gear too? My pick: yes.

188 of 291 wearable items are made. Rolls only on drops would leave Smithing, Fletching and Wizardry out of the best part.

2. Rolls instead of masterwork quality? My pick: yes, one system.

The Relic mockup proposed Fine/Superior/Masterwork quality. Rarity does the same job and is easier to read. Masterwork becomes "Jackpot, made by a named smith".

3. Start with the Dealer only? My pick: yes, phase 1.

One line, re-deal for tickets, no chips. Learn what players do with it before adding chips.

4. Up lines on weapons and armour at all? My pick: small ones.

Sideways-only is the safest for balance, but "+2 defence" is the line casual players understand best.

5. Tradeable? My pick: yes.

Rolled items are the best thing to trade in an ARPG. With 36 players it's a small market, but a real one.

6. Named effects stay unique? My pick: yes.

Execute, calm, no hospital bill and the rest never roll, so named items keep their place.

7. Tools and work clothes? My pick: not yet.

Tools reforge on their skill and work clothes have sets; rolls there would be a second, separate design.

8. Names

Lucky / Hot / Jackpot and the six chips are placeholders that fit the casino. Open to better ones.

Building it

  • Data: an item in the bag or bank already carries f (its reforge level and gem code) and a reforged piece never stacks. A rolled piece adds r: up to three short codes like "tix:3". Rolled pieces never stack either. Worn gear keeps its code the way eqf does today.
  • Rules: statOf adds Up lines; fxOf adds sideways lines under OUT_CAP. Every number in the game already reads through those two, so combat, the Character window and the buff bar pick rolls up with no other change. ROLLS holds the line table, and rollFor(item, seed) is one function the server uses for drops, crafts and chips.
  • Server: a roll at drop or craft time. A {t:"deal", op, i, chip} message at the Dealer. Trades and the bank carry r. Everything checked server-side; the page only draws.
  • Page: the coloured name and lines on every item tooltip, green ▲ / red ▼ compare against worn, the Dealer's window, loot highlighting.
  • Cost to run: nothing ticks. A save grows by a few bytes per rolled item.
  • Size: phase 1 about 4 days (rolls, the Dealer, tooltips, a test that drops, crafts, re-deals and checks the caps). Phase 2 about 3 days plus art for the chips (drafted above). Phase 3 about 2 days.
