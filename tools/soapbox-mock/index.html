Soapbox Racing

Build a kart out of junk, race it, smash it into everyone else's, and keep it running. The race below is playable: your kart against five, bumper-car physics, three laps. Every bump wears your parts and theirs; a part worn to nothing breaks (the wheels go, the steering pulls, the engine coughs), and you fix it in the garage with the Tinkering parts you already salvage. Better parts turn up from the Junk King, Thrill Hill's stunt crates and the races themselves.

Race it

Three tracks. The two circuits are far bigger than the screen: the camera follows you and looks ahead the way you are going, and the minimap shows the rest. The Junkyard Run goes through a tunnel under the scrap heap where nobody can see you (or what you do to them); the Figure Eight crosses itself on a bridge, and karts on the deck and karts underneath never touch. Leave the road and the gravel slows you; past it is a fence. Click the track and drive. Bump people: a hard hit knocks them sideways and wears their parts; your bumper takes the first of it. Your kart is whatever is fitted in the garage below, worn parts and all, so change a part and race again to feel the difference.

The garage

Five slots. Each part has stats, a rarity and a condition. Fit a part from your stash, repair one with scrap and gears (the Tinkering parts you already salvage), and tune the gearing. Everything here changes the kart you race above.

The loop

A day's soapbox is a race or three, a few minutes in the garage, and the occasional lucky find. The kart improves slowly because good parts are rare and every one wears out.

Find parts→Fit and tune→Race and bump→Parts wear→Repair, or it breaks→Win better parts↺
FindParts drop from race prizes (one a race, better for a podium), the Junk King and his yard, Thrill Hill's stunt crates and salvage at Sal's bench. Sal sells Junk parts for tickets so nobody is ever stuck.
Break inA new part reaches its full stats after its first 20 laps (it starts at 90%), so a Legendary is a project, not a switch. That is the "train it" part: a kart gets better the more you drive it.
WearEvery bump takes condition, the bumper first. A race also costs a little condition on every part. At 0 a part breaks and does a fraction of its job until fixed.
RepairAt Sal's bench for scrap and gears, Tinkering's own parts. A part loses 2% of its top condition with every repair, so even a Legendary is eventually scrapped for a few relics.
ShowA paint job and a number (cosmetics, from the Store), your kart parked outside your cottage, a weekly cup with a leaderboard and a title.
Never requiredIt pays in tickets, parts, titles and paint. Nothing from racing makes your character stronger, so it stays a hobby.

Race modes

Circuits (two playable above)Long tracks with hairpins, chicanes, a tunnel or a bridge, two or three laps, six karts, bumping allowed. A new track every season. A race every 15 minutes on the track at the Carnival; empty seats are filled with house karts. Prizes: tickets and a part.
Bumper Bash (playable above)Sixty seconds in an arena. Points for every hit, more for breaking someone's part. Pure bumper cars: the mode for people who don't care about lines.
Downhill time trialThrill Hill's road, against everyone's ghosts (a recording of their best run, so nothing to sync live). A weekly leaderboard with a title for first.
The Soapbox CupA Sunday-night event, CASINO calling it: heats, a final, a trophy for the island. The all-server moment.

Real multiplayer, bumper cars and all

You asked for people to actually race each other, so this is the part that matters. The world server ticks every 50 ms and every map shares it: a race does not belong there. Each race gets its own small room (a separate Durable Object, one per race, like the game's maps but just for six karts):

The room runs the physicsThe same code as this page's race, on the server, 30 times a second. Players send only their keys; the room decides every position and every bump, so nobody can drive through walls or say they hit harder.
Your own kart feels instantYour page runs your kart straight away from your keys and nudges it to the server's word when they disagree; the others are drawn a tenth of a second behind, smoothed. That's how every online racing game hides the delay.
What it costsSix karts × 30 updates a second is about 4 KB a second to each racer, and the inputs coming back are a few bytes. A race lasts a minute or two, then the room is gone. The world's tick never notices.
Bumps are fairA collision is resolved once, in the room, with both karts' real stats, then both players see the same bounce. On a bad connection you may see a bump a moment late; you never see one that didn't happen.
The honest risk: bumper-car racing over the internet is the hardest thing on this page to make feel good, harder than anything the game does today. The plan to keep it safe: build the race room first and test it with two of us on dev before anything else, and keep the downhill time trial (ghosts, no live sync) as the fallback that works on any connection. About 8-10 days for the room and the race; the garage and parts are about 3 more.
