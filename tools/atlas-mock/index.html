The atlas: every skill to 120, and where

The other mockups say what comes next and why. Almost all of it needs somewhere to happen: a level past 99 is nothing without a rock, a tree, a fishing spot, a mark or a course to spend it on, and those live on maps. This page measures where every skill's content is today, finds the holes, and lays out where every skill goes from here to 120: seven new maps (the four drawn in mockup 4, reshaped, plus three more), two built-and-held maps opened, and which skill feeds which. It's the base the rest of the content gets built on.

The atlas

Where each new place hangs off the world. Grey is open today, gold is built and held, green is new. The two gateways are the doors past 99: skilling-first maps at 98-106, each the way into an endgame map behind it, so a level-99 skiller has somewhere to go the week the curve opens.

The maps

Seven new places and two to open. Each one is a combat band and a set of skilling tiers, because the game's rule is that a fighter and a skiller stand in the same field. Levels on the skill chips are what a spot needs, not the monsters.

Every skill to 120

The top of each skill today, then one step every five levels to 120, each with its place. Gathering skills get a new resource; making skills get the recipes those resources open; the rest get places of their own. Red is a hole that exists today.

How it fits together

Measured from every recipe: how many recipes in the column skill take something the row skill produces. The big lines are Woodcutting into Fletching (42), Alchemy into Wizardry (47), Fishing into Cooking (40), and monsters' drops into almost everything.

Four skills are islands.Agility, Thieving, Breeding and Tinkering make almost nothing another skill needs, so nobody needs a thief, a runner or a breeder. Past 100 each gets a job: Thieving lifts the keys and charts that open places (the Vault Ghost's key, the Archive's catalogue), Agility opens the shortcuts the gathering spots sit behind, Breeding's pets carry the gatherers' bonuses (mockup 7), and Tinkering builds every map's projects.

Six chains past 110

Every 100+ resource is used by at least two other skills, so no high level is made alone.

Rules for a map

A tier every five levelsFrom 100 to 120 every gathering skill has a new resource at roughly 100, 105, 110, 115 and 118-120, so XP an hour keeps rising and there's always a next spot.
Two places per ten levelsNo band hangs on one map: a crowded rock in one place is a queue, two places is a choice. Each map has at least two spots of each resource it holds.
Fighters and skillers in one fieldAs the game already does: every new map has its monsters and its rocks, trees and water, with the gathering on the quieter side and the aggressive monsters on the far one.
A bank within a mapThe late maps have no banks today. Each gateway has a camp chest, and the High Strip is a full town, so no 100+ resource is more than one map from a bank.
A waystone eachLike the Frozen Reach's: every new map is one scroll away once you've been there.
Every resource feeds two skillsA 110 log, ore or fish is wanted by at least two other skills, so high levels trade with each other on the Exchange.
Its own art, stagedEach map ships with its ground, its props, three monsters and its resources first; the rest of its monsters and its boss follow.
No new skillsEighteen is the count, and every one of them has a ladder to 120 below.

Build order: the where first

This replaces the map lines of the game plan's roadmap: maps first, because everything else lands on them.

Things to decide: the seven names; whether the gateways have bosses (my pick: no, a gateway is for working, and the Mountain Rumbles event lands in the Ashlands); whether skillers can reach their spots in the 110+ maps without fighting (my pick: yes, each map's quiet side); whether the High Strip is a full town with every station (my pick: yes, it is the endgame's Yard); and whether Thieving stays in the Guild below 97 or also spreads to the maps (my pick: the Guild to 97, the maps past 100).
