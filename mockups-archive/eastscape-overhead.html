Overhead bars: health you can read, weaknesses you can see

Click a monster (or a player) to pick your target, then hit it. The bar carries the health as a number, the lost chunk flashes and drains so you can see a hit land, and the tiny icons over the head say what it's weak to and what it shrugs off. Hitting a weakness makes its icon pulse, so the game teaches it without a wiki.

What's on the plate

  • The number. Current health, in the bar itself (214 / 300). It counts down with the hit rather than snapping, and turns amber under half and red under a quarter.
  • The drain. What a hit took stays lit in pale gold for a moment, then drains away. Big hits read as big at a glance, even in a crowd.
  • The icons, over the name. Green with a ▲: weak to it (+35% damage). Red with a ▼: resists it (half damage). A style icon with a percent: it guards against that style (the Slag Golem shrugs off 40% of every arrow).
  • A pulse when it matters. Land a hit on a weakness and that icon pops and the splat says WEAK. Hit a resistance and it goes grey and says RESIST.
  • Players carry the same plate. They have no elemental weaknesses today, so their icon row shows what's protecting them: a Witch's ward, a page buff, Hematite toughness. In the Wilderness that is exactly what an attacker wants to know.

Decisions for you

  • Which style: A (a plate with the name on it) is the clearest in a crowd; B keeps the name under the feet where it is today; C is the smallest change.
  • Always, or learned? "Learned" hides a monster's icons until you've hit it with that element once (or read it in the wiki), then remembers them for good. It turns weaknesses into something you find, which suits a game about exploring. "Always" is friendlier to new players.
  • When the plate shows: today a bar appears only once something is hurt. The sketch shows plates all the time so you can compare; in the game I'd show them on anything in a fight, anything you hover, and your own when you're hurt.
